I am a Research Assistant Professor in the Department of Earth, Marine and Environmental
Sciences at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where I was a postdoctoral
scholar from 2019 to 2022. I hold a Ph.D. in environmental sciences from the University of
Puerto Rico.

My research develops geospatial AI and multi-sensor fusion methods for Earth observation. I
combine optical, radar, lidar, and imaging spectroscopy data from satellites, aircraft, and
drones with field measurements, and I build models that are interpretable and report their
uncertainty.

I apply these methods to forests, wetlands, and coastal water in the southeastern United
States: forest height and biomass with the USDA Forest Service, tree mortality and ghost
forests along the Atlantic coast, flooding during hurricanes and high tides, and water levels
in coastal sounds. This work has been supported by five NASA-funded projects.
