## Approach

Students learn geospatial data science most efficiently when the connections between
concepts, methods, and applications are clear. I begin each unit with a real task, such as
predicting urban flood risk, estimating vegetation structure from several sensors, or tracking
wetland change through time. Students then work through the same sequence each time: clarify
the decision the analysis serves, evaluate the data and its spatial dependence, choose or
adapt a method, implement it with good software practice, validate it, and say what the result
supports and where it can fail.

Reproducibility is part of every assignment. Students document assumptions, organize data and
metadata, use version control, and design train and test splits that respect spatial
autocorrelation. Early in a course I provide documentation, starter templates, and guided
exercises. As students gain confidence I reduce the scaffolding.

## Courses I would develop

- **GeoAI: machine and deep learning for geospatial data.** Supervised learning for mapping, semantic segmentation and object detection, spatiotemporal modeling, interpretability, spatial validation, and uncertainty.
- **High-performance geospatial computing.** Cloud platforms such as Google Earth Engine, parallel and GPU processing on research computing clusters, cloud-native data formats, and workflow design for regional to global analysis.
- **Multi-sensor remote sensing: SAR, lidar, and imaging spectroscopy.** Physical principles of each sensor, co-registration and harmonization, data fusion, and uncertainty propagation.

## Mentoring

At UNC I currently co-advise one MS student, advise three undergraduate researchers, and
serve on two PhD committees. Three MS students whom I co-advised or mentored have completed
their degrees. Former mentees have presented at the AGU Fall Meeting, and two are authors of
2026 papers in *Landscape Ecology*, one as first author.

At the start of a project, the student and I agree on goals, expected products, and
authorship. Early on I give detailed guidance on methods and coding practice. As the student
gains experience I move to open-ended questions and leave more of the decisions to them. I
adjust the amount of structure to each student and to each stage of a project.
